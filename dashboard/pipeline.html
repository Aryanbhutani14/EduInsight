Where Hadoop and Spark are used

Every heavy stage reads from and writes to HDFS, and is a Spark job. Only the final search box (one small lookup) runs outside Spark. Modules 2, 3 and 4 were also run on all 59,581 videos; see the Full Corpus page.

StageHadoop (HDFS)SparkScript
IngestionStores the 7 source files: 949 MB for the full corpus, 69 MB for the CS subset-wsl_ingest_full.sh
PreprocessingReads captions, writes cleaned word lists as ParquetSplits captions across parallel partitions, tokenizes in parallel, joins concepts to videos, computes TF-IDF with distributed aggregationsspark_module2_preprocess.py
EmbeddingsReads cleaned captions, writes passage and lecture vectorsRuns the GPU language model inside each Spark partition, then pools vectors per lecture with a group-byspark_module3_embed.py
Clustering and similarityReads vectors, writes clusters and neighbour tablesMLlib K-means; a broadcast-and-partition job finds each lecture's 10 nearest neighboursspark_module4_cluster.py
Ranking and evaluationReads vectors, writes score tablesScores every test query against every passage in parallel; BM25 keyword search written with Spark DataFramesspark_module5_rank.py
Search boxVectors exported from HDFS to local filesNot used: one in-memory lookup that answers in about 76 mseduinsight_search.py
Honest scope: HDFS runs on one machine (pseudo-distributed: one DataNode, replication 1) and Spark runs in local mode, using 8 to 20 parallel tasks on one laptop. Hadoop MapReduce and YARN are not used: HDFS does the storage and Spark does the processing, which is the standard modern setup. The same code would run on a real cluster by changing the master address and the HDFS address.

How the search engine works

In plain language, from raw captions to a ranked answer.

  1. Offline, once (Hadoop and Spark): each lecture's captions are cleaned and cut into about 12 short passages.
  2. Offline, once (Spark with a GPU): a multilingual language model turns every passage into a list of 768 numbers, a "meaning fingerprint". Passages with similar meaning get similar numbers, even across English and Chinese. These are saved in HDFS.
  3. Online, per search: the query becomes a fingerprint too and is compared with every passage; each lecture is scored by its best-matching passage. This gives ranking A.
  4. Online, per search: the query is also matched against about 27,000 known Chinese concept names to find the closest Chinese terms, and a classic keyword search (BM25, which scores by word frequency) runs on those terms. This gives ranking B.
  5. Online, per search: the two rankings are merged (each list gives a lecture 1 / (60 + rank) points) and the top lectures are returned with the passage that matched.
Why it sometimes fails: the language model is a general one and can miss technical terms, and the English-to-Chinese concept matching sometimes picks a wrong word. The evaluation on the CS Subset & EDA page measures how often.

Module 4 — Similarity & Clustering
Tanmay Sangwan
  • Spark MLlib K-means (cosine distance) over the video vectors; k=12 chosen from a sweep of 9 values by silhouette score
  • Distributed Spark job (broadcast + mapPartitions) finds the 10 most similar videos for every video
  • Cross-course redundancy: candidate pairs checked against real word overlap and read by hand
  • t-SNE 2-D map of all 4,071 videos; results written to HDFS as Parquet
Deliverable: clustering module — 12 topic clusters, a similarity table, and redundancy findings, in 52 s
Verified: every cluster mixes at least 9 courses (topics, not course boundaries). 447 cross-course video pairs score ≥0.90 and are the same topic taught by different lecturers (e.g. IP addressing in two networking courses). Limits: silhouette is low (~0.11) and clusters from other random seeds agree only moderately (ARI 0.57–0.65). Details and the cluster map are on the CS Subset & EDA page.
