Data cleaning

What actually happened between the raw MOOCCubeX files and this subset — every step traceable to a real transformation, not a black box.

  1. Bridged resource_id ↔ ccid using the found video_id-ccid.txt file
    2,798,892 mapping rows applied
  2. Restricted to Computer-Science-tagged courses via course-field.json
    3,781 courses → 74 CS-tagged
  3. Filtered to courses with ≥10 captioned videos — dropped courses with too little usable text
    74 → 59 courses kept (15 dropped)
  4. Restricted video.json to only ccids referenced by kept courses, deduplicating shared videos
    59,581 → 4,071 videos
  5. Restricted concept files to entries touching kept videos/courses
    637,572 → 27,552 concepts
  6. Flagged (not silently removed) generic/noisy concepts — ≤2-character terms like "行" (row) or "类" (class) — for stopword-style filtering in the NLP stage, not deleted from the record
    786 of 27,552 concepts flagged (2.9%)

Concept quality: raw frequency vs. after filtering

why step 6 above is necessary — confirmed by inspecting raw source records, not assumed

Only 2.9% of the 27,552 concepts are short generic words — but they dominate raw frequency rankings because common words appear everywhere. 97.1% are genuine multi-character technical terms (e.g. 伪多项式时间算法 = "pseudo-polynomial time algorithm"). Two items above (marked *) are themselves raw extraction artifacts from MOOCCubeX's own concept-extraction pipeline — "程序运*" is a truncated "程序运行" (program runtime), and "寄存器，*" carries a stray trailing comma — left unedited to show the data as-is.

The 27% of nearest neighbours in a different course are exactly the cross-course similarity Module 4 will analyse.

Clustering and similarity (Module 4)

Spark MLlib K-means groups the 4,071 video vectors into topics, and a distributed Spark job finds the 10 most similar videos for every video. Cross-course pairs that look redundant were then checked against real word overlap and read by hand. 52 s end to end.

Map of all 4,071 lectures

each dot is one video, placed by t-SNE so that similar lectures sit close together. Hover a dot for its topic and course. Distances between far-apart groups on a t-SNE map are not meaningful; only local neighbourhoods are.

One panel per cluster, highlighted in blue. The silhouette score (about 0.11) means topic groups overlap, which is typical for lecture text, so k = 12 is a reasonable choice rather than a uniquely optimal one, and clusterings from other seeds agree only moderately. All 12 clusters contain videos from at least 9 courses, so they follow topics rather than course boundaries. Exceptions: trees (80% from one Data Structures course) and urban planning (66% from one course). Data Structures (Part 1) alone is 12% of the subset.

Is high similarity real overlap?

mean share of distinct words two lectures have in common, by similarity of the pair

Word overlap rises steadily with similarity, from 0.037 for random cross-course pairs to 0.167 at ≥0.95. It stays low in absolute terms, so these are different lecturers covering the same topic, not copied lectures.
