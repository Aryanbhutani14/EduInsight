Scale-up — Modules 2, 3 and 4 on all 59,581 videos

The full corpus on Spark

The same three Spark jobs that were built and checked on the 4,071-video CS subset were re-run, unchanged in logic, on every captioned video in MOOCCubeX (949 MB of text in HDFS). This page reports what happened at that scale. Everything ran on one laptop in Spark local mode with 8 worker threads, so it shows that the code scales with partitions, not that we have a cluster.

Subset versus full corpus

Same code, 14.6 times the videos. Run time grew much more slowly than the data, partly because a Spark job has a fixed start-up cost that dominates the small run.

Does a video's nearest neighbour sit in its own course?

chance that the closest video (by embedding) is from the same course

It drops from 73% to 37.2% at full scale. A likely cause is that the full corpus holds many parallel courses on the same subject (several calculus and several Chinese-literature courses), so the closest video is often a near-equivalent lecture from another course. We have not proven that this is the whole explanation.

Topic clusters across all disciplines

K-means (cosine, Spark MLlib) was run for k = 10 to 60 and the best silhouette score picked k = 60. t-SNE can only draw a few thousand points, so the map shows a random sample of 8,000 videos; the clustering itself used all 59,512.

Silhouette score by k

higher means tighter, better-separated clusters

Honest reading: all scores are low (0.12–0.13), and the best k is at the edge of the range we tried, so a larger k might score slightly higher. Other random seeds agree with this clustering only moderately (adjusted Rand index 0.58–0.62). Treat the clusters as a useful browsing aid, not a ground truth.

Every cluster mixes videos from many courses (smallest: 45 courses), and in no cluster does one course make up more than 24% of the videos. Clusters group by subject, not by course.

Redundant coverage across courses

Each video's 10 nearest neighbours were found with a blockwise cosine join in Spark. Pairs from different courses with very high similarity are candidates for redundant lectures. Counts are lower bounds, because only each video's top 10 neighbours are searched.

Videos with a similar partner in another course

number of videos involved, by cosine similarity threshold

At ≥0.90, 20,918 videos (35% of the corpus) have a partner in a different course.

Is high similarity real overlap?

mean share of distinct words two lectures have in common, by similarity of the pair

Word overlap rises steadily with similarity, from 0.022 for random cross-course pairs to 0.182 at ≥0.95, so the embedding similarity does track shared content.

Which course pairs overlap most

number of video pairs with similarity ≥0.90, for each pair of courses

A finding we did not expect: the biggest overlaps are mostly the same course published twice under different course IDs (for example a law course and its “Tsinghua-certified” version) or videos that belong to no course at all. The top pairs have cosine 1.0 and word overlap 1.0, meaning identical transcripts. In a recommender these must be de-duplicated, otherwise the same lecture would fill several of the top 5 slots.

What this run does and does not show

  • Shows: the Spark code handles 59,581 videos, 93 million tokens and 771,474 embedded chunks end to end, from HDFS input to HDFS output, in about 30 minutes.
  • Does not show: multi-node speed-up. This is one machine in local mode, so the evidence is correctness and memory-safe design (blockwise neighbour search, Arrow batches, sampled validation), not cluster scaling.
  • Not re-evaluated: the Module 5 ranking evaluation (82 queries, hand-judged) was done on the CS subset only. Quality numbers on that page do not transfer automatically to the full corpus.
  • Not comparable: the “nearest neighbour in the same course” rate and the cluster silhouette differ from the subset because the data and k differ.
  • Labels are ours: cluster names and translated titles on this page were written by hand and have not been checked by a native Chinese reader.
